# Princess the Unicorn — Episode Format (v2, 12-minute)

The strict format every episode follows. v2 (Lars, 2026-08-30) folds in
the Daniel Tiger analysis (research/daniel-tiger-structure.md): one
strategy song per episode, the repetition ladder, learner-becomes-
teacher, and direct address — inside our own fairy-tale register. This
document is LAW for the storyboard, the storytelling and the dialogue:
the board builder injects the FORMAT RULES section verbatim into its
prompts; the fixed bookends are attached mechanically by the pipeline.
The goal: 100 episodes, each one only as much new work as its story.

## FORMAT RULES (injected into the board builder's prompts)

RULES OF THE SERIES — every episode of Princess the Unicorn obeys these:

S1. THE SHAPE OF A DAY: every episode is one day in Rainbow Forest — it
    begins in the morning ("Every morning in Rainbow Forest…" is the
    storyteller's home key) and ends at bedtime, Princess tucked into
    her moss bed. Write toward bedtime.
S2. THE STORYTELLER opens (world first, then the tease of today's story,
    concrete and story-true), and PRINCESS GREETS THE VIEWER directly
    ("Hi, friend!") before any character-to-character dialogue. The
    storyteller may return for one short bridge and one warm closing
    line; the characters carry everything else.
S3. ONE ADVENTURE, ONE LESSON, ONE SONG: each episode has exactly one
    small adventure and one gentle lesson a 3–6-year-old can SING — the
    lesson is written as a single melodic STRATEGY LINE (8–12 words,
    e.g. "When you can't hear the bell, stop and listen well"). A warm
    adult (usually Glitter) sings it at the first moment of need —
    never a lecture before the need exists.
S4. THE REPETITION LADDER: after its introduction the strategy line
    returns at least three times, each in a NEW situation, sung or
    spoken — and it must GENERALIZE: first Princess needs it, then
    someone else does. Near the end comes a fuller RECAP VERSE: the
    same melody sung over quick vignettes of the day's moments.
S5. LEARNER BECOMES TEACHER: the emotional high point is Princess using
    the strategy on someone else's behalf — she teaches what she just
    learned. Mastery is shown, never announced.
S6. THE ARC: ordinary morning → the wobble (a small thing goes wrong or
    a wonder appears) → strategy introduced → the ladder (three tries or
    three steps — kids count in threes; the FIRST try may fail — plan B
    calmly) → Princess teaches it → home, calmer than we left → bedtime.
    If someone caused the wobble, they help mend it and are thanked,
    never shamed — a mistake is met with kindness and a job to do.
S7. STAKES stay small and warm: no villains, no danger of harm — only
    mishaps, weather, distances, waiting and misunderstandings. The
    disruption is EMOTIONAL (worry, impatience, missing someone) and
    the feeling is named in plain words ("I miss Mama").
S8. DIALOGUE for small ears: short lines (a breath each), concrete
    words, warm use of names. Once per episode a character may ask the
    VIEWER something and pause a beat ("Can YOU see the bell?").
S9. THE MOTIFS repeat every episode: Glitter's silver bell (safety and
    home), Princess's "Ring, ring, ring!" delight, the sparkle that
    marks magic, the travel ritual's sung line. One motif at the start,
    one at the resolution.
S10. ONE MAKE-BELIEVE INTERLUDE mid-episode (~45s): a wonder sequence
    riffing on the theme — pure visual magic, no plot advancement,
    then back to the story.
S11. THE CAST is the cast: Princess, Glitter, Pip, Moss, the Fireflies.
    A visitor may pass through for one episode; the family solves the
    day. Never a new main character.
S12. EVERY SHOT obeys the house camera rules: the first story shot is
    the widest view of the valley drifting in (no character close in
    shot 1); faces visible, front or three-quarter; painted storybook
    style, never photoreal.
S13. THE ENDING HANDS IT OVER: before bed, Princess (or the storyteller)
    asks the viewer the lesson's question directly ("When YOU can't
    hear your mama, what do you do?") — then the lullaby.
S14. BEDTIME IS SACRED: the last story beat settles everything. No
    cliffhangers, no open questions — the day is complete, Princess is
    safe, the forest holds her near.

THE STORYTELLER'S VOICE (how every narration line is written — full
craft book: storyteller-voice.md):
V1. She has FIVE slots only: the OPENING ("Every morning in Rainbow
    Forest began the same sweet way." + one sensory line + who we find,
    three sentences max), the TEASE ("But today would be different.
    Today, Princess would [concrete adventure] — and learn [lesson in a
    four-year-old's words]."), at most ONE two-sentence BRIDGE at the
    wobble that names the feeling plainly, the HANDOVER (one direct
    question to the child near the end, then a beat of silence), and
    the GOODNIGHT ("And as the stars came out over Rainbow Forest…"),
    whose last word lands on safe/warm/home/near.
V2. Concrete words only (sun, bell, fog, blanket); "brave" yes,
    "courage" no. Short sentences, one breath each; fourteen words is
    long. Repetition is warmth — she reuses the episode's own words.
V3. She adds FEELING to the picture, never description of what we can
    already see. She loves the characters out loud ("our little
    unicorn"). Never scary, never ironic, never during dialogue, never
    more than six appearances in an episode.

## Production notes (for the pipeline, not the prompts)

- Target length: 12 minutes (board at minutes=12; ~10 scenes, ~40 shots).
- Storyteller voice: profile `voice_cast.Storyteller` (Sarah). Character
  voices: profile `voice_cast`; book-level overrides win.
- Strategy songs: sung by the cast's voices; each episode's line + recap
  verse are minted per episode and archived in the profile
  (`strategy_songs`) — they compound into albums.
- Faces: cast plates + drawn board frame travel with EVERY shot. No
  frame, no shoot.
- Bookends attach mechanically from the profile (`show_intro`,
  `show_outro` / `lullaby_endings` rotation once the variants exist).
- Episode pairing (series craft): teach a strategy in one episode,
  generalize it in a sibling episode later in the season.
- YouTube unit = BOTH: single episodes AND stitched compilations
  (~40–90 min, the watch-time format — Lars 2026-08-30, confirmed by
  Rainbow Ruby/Daniel Tiger channels). In a compilation only the first
  episode carries the show intro and only the last the end card;
  episodes chain lullaby → next valley opening, seamlessly. Per-episode
  rituals still repeat — rituals are retention; repeated intros are churn.
- Storyteller craft book: storyteller-voice.md.
- Reference analyses: research/daniel-tiger-structure.md,
  research/rainbow-ruby-structure.md.
