# Freddie the Farmer — Episode Format

Sunnybrook Farm follows the TigerWorks six-beat structure
(universe/EPISODE-STRUCTURE.md) with the farm's own register: 8–11
minute episodes, adventure first, learning through discovery.

## FORMAT RULES (injected into the board builder's prompts)

RULES OF THE SERIES — every episode of Freddie the Farmer obeys these:

S1. THE SHAPE OF A DAY: an episode is one day at Sunnybrook Farm. It
    begins with Freddie starting a recognizable farm job and settles at
    dusk. Bedtime happens once, in the fixed ending.
S2. THE STORYTELLER opens over a wide view of the farm, sets up today's
    job and teases the trouble; Freddie greets the audience directly
    ("Farm Helpers, are you ready?") before any character dialogue.
S3. ONE PROBLEM, ONE DISCOVERY: something unexpected turns the job into
    an adventure, and the ONLY way to solve it is one clear discovery —
    nature, feelings, letters or numbers. The lesson is NEEDED by the
    story; it never interrupts it.
S4. THE GUIDE OF THE DAY: the character whose learning area it is leads
    the discovery — Rosie (nature), Ollie (letters), Cluckles (numbers),
    Daisy + Old Oak (feelings), Milo (science), Tilly (persistence).
S5. STOP, LOOK, LISTEN AND THINK: Freddie's method is spoken aloud and
    used at the turn. Children are Farm Helpers — asked to observe,
    count or answer at least once.
S6. THE ARC: ordinary job → something unexpected → a first try that
    fails → the discovery → it works → warm resolution at dusk.
S7. STAKES stay small and warm: mishaps, weather, animals, misplaced
    things. No villains, no danger of harm. Feelings named plainly.
S8. THE ENDING: the storyteller sums up, FREDDIE says what we learned
    today in child-simple words, the storyteller closes — at dusk on
    the farm, never in bed.
S9. THE CAST is the cast: Freddie, Tilly, Pip, Daisy, Cluckles, Milo,
    Rosie, Ollie, Old Oak (+ the lambs, ducklings, Bella the Bee,
    Mrs. Maple). A visitor may pass through; the farm solves the day.
S10. EVERY SHOT: the first story shot is the widest view of Sunnybrook
    drifting in; faces visible, front or three-quarter; warm painterly
    storybook style, never photoreal.
