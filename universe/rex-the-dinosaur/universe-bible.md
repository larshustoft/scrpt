# Rex the Dinosaur — Universe Bible (v1, 2026-09-09)

**Thesis.** Rex is the little dinosaur who shows children that being small is no reason to think small. Every story starts with something Rex notices and ends with something he understands — and a roar.

**Tone.** Warm, funny, brave. Short sentences. One idea per page. Rex's little arms are a running joke he is proud of, never a sadness.

**World — Fern Valley.** Giant ferns, Ripple River, round boulders, big flowers, and Puff the Volcano, who only ever puffs a small friendly cloud. Nothing in the valley is dangerous; things are big, new and worth a closer look.

**Cast.**
- **Rex** (lead). Young T. rex, moss green, cream belly, amber eyes, red bandana. Curious first, brave second, kind always. Catchphrase: "Roar! Let's find out."
- **Dot the Triceratops.** Lilac-grey, peach frill, yellow flower behind the frill. The planner and counter. Teaches counting, sorting, shapes.
- **Skye the Pterodactyl.** Sky-blue wings, orange beak, goggles on her head. The scout. Teaches looking closely, maps, up/down/near/far.
- **Bramble the Ankylosaurus.** Brown armour, club tail, a leaf on his back. Gentle strength, shy. Teaches patience and trying again.
- **Professor Fern.** Old brachiosaurus with half-moon spectacles. Names every plant and star. Teaches nature, science and letters.

**Learning pillars.** Counting & shapes (Dot) · Nature & science (Professor Fern) · Letters & sounds (Professor Fern, Skye) · Feelings & courage (Rex, Bramble) · Trying again (Bramble).

**Formats.** Picture books (8.5 x 11, 32 pages, read-aloud, ages 3-6) · Activity and workbooks (8.5 x 11, black-and-white, ages 4-8: counting, mazes, dot-to-dot, how-to-draw) · Later: short films and songs, as with Princess the Unicorn.

**Rules for every artist and writer.** Draw every character exactly like the plates. Rex leads every cover. Text only on clear fields, never over a face. No scary dinosaurs, no chases, no teeth as threat. The valley is a place children want to live in.

**Pen name.** Bennie Clay.
