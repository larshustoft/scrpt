# HOW A SEASON WORKS — the house format

Applies to every children's universe. A season is not a pile of episodes;
it is a block that can be produced, released and watched as a unit — while
every single episode still stands completely on its own.

## The two rules that must both hold

**1. Any episode is a child's first episode.** No episode may require
another. The rule is re-taught inside the story every time. A returning
friend introduces themself in one line ("I'm Moss — I'm afraid of nearly
everything"). The finale explains its own stakes from scratch.

**2. A season rewards the child who stays.** Continuity is carried by
things a returning viewer *notices* and a new viewer never misses: a
growing cast, a changing world, and a collection.

## The four things that make a season a season

**The season question.** One developmental theme, asked twelve to
twenty-four times in different clothes. Season one of Princess is *"Can I
manage what I feel?"* — every episode is a feeling met and survived.

**The growing cast.** Two or three new friends per season. Each gets one
episode where they arrive and are properly introduced; afterwards they
appear in the background of other episodes without explanation. A child
who has watched them all sees a fuller forest; a child watching one sees
a full forest anyway.

**The turning year.** A season sits in one stretch of the year. Season one
runs spring into summer; season two runs autumn into winter and out the
other side. Children mark time by weather, and it gives the films visual
variety without changing the world.

**The collection.** Something visible accumulates. Princess earns her own
silver bell in episode one; from then on each adventure adds one small
charm to the ribbon. By the finale the bell carries twelve — a record of
the season a child can see and count, that never once needs explaining.

## The shape of a season

| Episode | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | The **origin** of the season's question, and the strongest single story. It must work as the only episode anyone ever sees. |
| 2–4 | The question in easy clothes. Small, warm, funny. Establish the rhythm. |
| 5 | **New friend one** arrives. |
| 6–8 | Harder feelings. The first episode where the lesson costs something. |
| 9 | **New friend two** arrives. |
| 10–11 | The learner becomes the teacher: Princess helps somebody else use the rule. |
| 12 | **The finale**: the season's question at its widest, every recurring friend on screen, and the collection complete. Still standalone. |

Longer seasons (18 or 24) repeat the 5/9 arrival beat and the
learner-becomes-teacher pair, and add one **special**: a double-length
episode at the turn of the year.

## Release

Singles weekly. Every three episodes, a compilation (~33 minutes) — that
is where the watch time lives. At season end, the full-season compilation
and the season's book box. Books release in episode order; the audiobook
of each is the film's own soundtrack.
