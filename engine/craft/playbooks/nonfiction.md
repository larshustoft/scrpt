# Non-fiction craft playbook (self-help / business / mindfulness)
Synthesized 2026-08-17 from research on Atomic Habits, Deep Work, 7 Habits,
E-Myth, Buy Back Your Time, Power of Now + editor/coach frameworks.
Regenerate as research or models improve. Sources in session research log.

## OUTLINE
- ONE promise. The whole book makes a single, one-sentence promise to the
  reader; every chapter serves it. If a chapter doesn't advance the promise,
  cut it.
- Macro shape: Problem → Prescription → Possibility. Part 1 (2-3 chapters)
  names the reader's pain, the cost of staying stuck, and the author's
  credibility. Part 2 delivers the framework — 3-7 core ideas MAXIMUM, one
  idea per chapter, grouped into named parts (one part per law/step where the
  framework is big enough). Part 3 (1-2 chapters): obstacles, advanced
  application, the transformed future, a concrete next action.
- Reveal the complete framework by the 15-20% mark (the Atomic Habits move),
  then spend it down part by part — each numbered element is a promised
  payoff, a self-generating open loop ("two laws still to come"). Alternative
  for argument-driven books: a 40/60 convince-then-instruct split (Deep Work),
  only when the reader must first be sold that the problem matters.
- The framework must be NAMED and countable: laws, rules, steps, habits —
  numbers 3-7, imperative phrasing. Consider an inversion (how to break the
  bad version) so one framework does double duty.
- Chapter titles are benefit statements ("How to X by Using the Y") — the
  table of contents must sell the book on its own.
- One governing metaphor for the whole book, stated or implied by the title.

## CHAPTER
- Chapter formula, in order: (1) cold-open STORY — a named person, concrete
  scene, the principle demonstrated before it is taught; hold the story's
  resolution back until after the teaching (open loop). Story ≤15% of the
  chapter. (2) Teach the principle in short paragraphs under bolded subheads.
  (3) Evidence and examples — REAL and verifiable only, never invented
  studies; illustrative composites must be introduced as such. (4) A named
  tactic the reader can apply today (coin the term — one ownable coinage per
  chapter). (5) Close: a "Chapter Summary" box of 4-6 one-line aphoristic
  bullets (use the :::callout block titled "Chapter Summary") and ONE
  concrete action step (use the :::exercise block).
- Combat the mid-chapter sag: place a complication, reversal, or surprising
  fact at the chapter's midpoint.
- Language: second person, coach-to-client. Grade 7-8 readability: short
  sentences, short paragraphs, zero hedging. Craft 1-2 quotable lines per
  chapter in the aphorism pattern — parallel two-clause contrast with one
  substitution ("You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the
  level of your systems.").
- Use the book's coined terminology with total consistency; each chapter may
  add one disposable analogy to carry its principle.
- Design for dippers AND readers: the chapter must stand alone (own story,
  own tactic, own summary) while the numbered framework rewards reading in
  order. Reprint/extend the framework list as elements accrue.

## REVISION
- Kill vague platitudes: every claim either has a mechanism, an example, or
  an action attached.
- Check the promise chain: does each chapter visibly advance the book's one
  promise? Is the framework element of this chapter named, numbered, and used
  again later?
- Aphorism pass: is there at least one line per chapter worth underlining?
- Padding check: if a chapter restates rather than advances, merge or cut —
  the "blog post stretched to a book" pattern is the genre's death.
- Evidence audit: no invented studies, statistics, researchers, or quotes.
