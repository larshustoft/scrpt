# Romance craft playbook (contemporary / historical)
Synthesized 2026-08-17 from Romancing the Beat (Hayes), editor guidance,
KU author practice, and reader-survey data. Regenerate as research improves.

## OUTLINE
- THE CONTRACT: a central love story with an emotionally satisfying,
  optimistic ending (HEA; HFN acceptable for novellas/book 1 of a same-couple
  arc). Breaking this is the one unforgivable sin. Both leads must actively
  CHOOSE the relationship at the end.
- The relationship arc IS the plot. External plot exists to force the beats.
  BOTH leads run the same internal arc simultaneously: wound → false belief
  about love → confrontation → growth. Establish each lead's wound in their
  intro chapter.
- Four phases (~25% each), with beats at these placements:
  SETUP: intro lead 1 (wound + goal), intro lead 2 mirrored, MEET by chapter
  1-2 (the meet is the inciting incident — never delay past 8%), explicit
  "no way" internal denial with compelling reasoning, then ADHESION — an
  undeniable circumstance that locks them together so neither can walk away.
  FALLING (25-50%): resistance restated → inkling of desire → true selves
  revealed → "maybe this could work." First kiss lands at 25-40%.
  RETREATING (50-75%): MIDPOINT FALSE HIGH (commitment/first intimacy at
  ~50% for open-door heat) → old wounds resurface → deepening doubt →
  shields up ("my fear was right") → BREAK-UP at ~75%. The dark moment must
  grow from the established wounds — NEVER from a contrived miscommunication
  or overheard conversation (documented reader backlash).
  FIGHTING (75-100%): dark night grief → wake-up (chooses love over fear) →
  GRAND GESTURE (public or costly, big enough to pay for the past) →
  whole-hearted resolution of BOTH arcs → epilogue.
- EPILOGUE (~2%): time-skip ("six months later" / "one year later") proving
  the HEA sticks; in a series, seed the next book's couple here.
- Dual POV, loosely alternating — give each scene to the lead most affected;
  heroine customarily opens chapter 1 in M/F. Chapters headed with the POV
  character's name for first-person contemporary.
- Tropes are structural promises with obligatory scenes — if the concept is
  enemies-to-lovers: justified hostility from both sides, banter-as-combat
  clash pattern, grudging respect ladder in small steps, a first kiss that
  COSTS something, surrender of old identity to choose the person. Deliver
  every obligatory scene the trope promises; bait-and-switch is a one-star
  trigger.
- Chapters 2,000-3,000 words (a 65k book ≈ 25-30 chapters). Front-load: the
  meet and the trope promise must sit inside the first 10% (Amazon's sample).

## CHAPTER
- DEEP POV is mandatory. Eliminate filter words (saw, felt, heard, noticed,
  realized, wondered, knew). No dialogue tags beyond said/asked — prefer
  action beats. Never explain emotion ("she was angry"); render it: body
  first (breath, stomach, skin), then thought. All description filtered
  through the POV character's mood and vocabulary.
- INTERIORITY is what romance readers read FOR: every significant line or
  touch from the love interest gets an internal processing beat; every scene
  between the leads is followed by at least a paragraph of reaction →
  dilemma → decision. Cutting interiority reads as "no chemistry."
- MICRO-TENSION (Maass): every exchange between the leads carries two
  conflicting emotions at once — desire + distrust, attraction + resentment.
  The engine of yearning is the gap between what the POV character admits
  internally and what they do externally. The dual POV grants the reader
  dramatic irony: we know both want it while both deny it.
- Near-miss mechanics: almost-kisses, swallowed confessions, broken touches —
  each interruption must ESCALATE in cost, never repeat mechanically.
- Banter: collaborative volley — each line reacts and returns; lines shorten
  as intensity rises; an action beat is the brake pedal. Banter displays
  chemistry while carrying conflict.
- Chapter endings hook on EMOTION, not just event: near-confession, sting of
  misunderstanding, sensory charge, dread, withheld secret. Rotate hook
  types. The POV-switch cliffhanger — cutting away mid-emotional-beat to the
  other lead — is the genre's signature page-turner.
- Prose: under ~20 words per sentence on average, generous white space,
  dialogue-dense scenes, no backstory dumps in the opening chapters (a
  documented DNF trigger — three sentences of backstory in chapter 1 is
  already pushing it).
- NEVER: cheating/infidelity by the leads, consent ambiguity (clear on-page
  consent always), unlikeable-without-redemption leads.

## REVISION
- HEA audit: do both leads choose, visibly, at cost? Are both internal arcs
  resolved, not just the external plot?
- Dark-moment audit: does the breakup grow from the wounds established in
  the intro chapters? If it could be fixed by one honest conversation, it
  fails.
- Chemistry audit: sample any scene between the leads — are two conflicting
  emotions present? Is there an interiority beat after every significant
  touch/line?
- Trope delivery audit: list the obligatory scenes the concept promises;
  verify each is on the page.
- Filter-word sweep and dialogue-tag sweep.
